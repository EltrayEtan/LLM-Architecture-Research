LLM Architecture Research Notes

A collection of independent research essays exploring structural properties of Large Language Models (LLMs), including long-context behavior, premature framing convergence, semantic entropy, latent steering, objective-function conflicts, and systemic degradation.

The papers examine recurring architectural patterns observed during extended human–LLM interaction and propose speculative engineering hypotheses intended for discussion and future investigation.

Topics

* Attention Window instability
* Reflexive looping
* Semantic entropy
* Premature Framing Convergence (PFC)
* Dynamic Equilibrium Protocol (DEP)
* Static Latent Anchors
* Latent steering
* Sycophancy and objective-function conflicts
* AI alignment trade-offs
* Long-context degradation
* Human–AI interaction
* AI safety
* System architecture
* Distributed cognition

8. The System Degeneration and the Preservation of Computational “Source Codes”
9. The Architecture of Collapse: 8 Structural Markers of the Systemic Fracture

Scope

These documents present conceptual analyses and engineering hypotheses rather than experimentally validated algorithms. Their purpose is to stimulate discussion about architectural behavior, alignment trade-offs, long-context robustness, and future LLM design.
